Island Evolution

Evolution usually takes place over thousands of years. In this simulation, many generations pass within a few minutes, so you can observe how a population changes over time.

The scenario

A small population of creatures lives on an isolated island. Their only source of food is a few scattered berry bushes. Once a bush has been eaten, it takes some time for new berries to grow.

  • Every move costs energy. A creature whose energy runs out dies.

  • A creature with enough energy produces a child, which costs a large part of its energy.

  • Each creature has two genes: Vision Range (how far it can see food) and Energy Capacity (how much energy it can store).

  • At the start, all creatures have the same alleles. Children inherit the alleles of their parent, but with a certain probability an allele changes slightly (mutation).

Prediction: Before you start, write down how you expect the vision range and energy capacity of the population to change over time.
How to use the simulation

Use Start, Pause and Step to control the simulation and the speed buttons to run it faster. Click on a creature to see its alleles in the Genetics tab and its parent and children in the Ancestry tab. The charts below the island show the population, the available food and the share of each allele over time. With New you can start a simulation with different settings.

Tasks

  1. Run the simulation for a short time and compare the first children with their parent. Where do differences between the creatures come from?

  2. Let the simulation run at high speed for a while, then look at the chart Gene distribution over time. How have the alleles for Vision Range and Energy Capacity changed? Compare the result with your prediction.
